1 · what this module does

Android switches the GPS receiver off the moment no app is asking for a position. Switch from your navigation app to a message for a minute and the satellites are gone; back in the map you wait for a new fix. Some ROMs (HyperOS, MIUI) cut the receiver even faster to save power.

GPS Locker holds the receiver on while you need it. Once it has the satellites it keeps them, so every app that asks for a position gets one at once: Google Maps, OsmAnd, Waze, Sygic and anything else.

It is meant for when you drive or travel: switch it on, switch it off when you arrive.

RootSukiSU Ultra or KernelSU (WebUI built in). Magisk works too - open the WebUI with MMRL or KSU WebUI Standalone.

2 · quick start
  1. Flash the zip. The small companion app is installed at the same time.
  2. Quick Settings → Edit → add the GPS Locker tile.
  3. Tap the tile. A notification shows the satellites, for example 14/22 sats locked.
  4. Tap the tile again to release the GPS.
HyperOS / MIUI: the system may block location for the new app. The WebUI then shows a warning, and the Action button opens the right settings page. Allow Location All the time and Notifications once. See section 9.
3 · how the lock works
you tap the tile
↓
companion app asks for a GPS position every second
↓
the receiver stays on and keeps tracking its satellites
↓
every navigation app gets a ready position

The request goes straight to the GPS chip, not through Google Play services, so it works with or without them.

The companion app has no icon and no screen. It is only a background service, the tile and the notification. It has no internet permission and sends nothing anywhere.

Faster first fix

How fast the first position comes depends on the assistance data the chip has: predicted satellite orbits (XTRA / PSDS) and the exact time. Android downloads them by itself. Refresh A-GPS in Tools asks for a fresh copy right now. GPS Locker does not replace your gps.conf: the vendor file is tuned for your chip, and copies from other phones do more harm than good.

4 · tile, notification, action
TileOn / off. While on, the subtitle shows 14/22 sats or Searching.
NotificationSatellites in the fix, accuracy, how many have L5, and per system (GPS, GLO, BDS, GAL). Stop releases the GPS.
ActionThe Action button of the module in your root manager does the same as the tile and prints the result.
WebUIThe big LOCK button, plus everything below.

Clear all in recent apps: on HyperOS / MIUI the module locks the app the same way as Lock on a card in recents, so Clear all leaves the GPS lock running. This works from the first reboot after the install. On other ROMs, if the system kills the app, Android starts it again after a short pause; the chip still remembers the satellites, so the lock comes back quickly.

5 · reading the numbers
Satellitesin the fix / with a signal. Each satellite counts once.
SignalsThe same, per signal. A dual-frequency phone receives many satellites twice (L1 and L5), so this number is higher.
L5 satsSatellites heard on the second band too (GPS L5, Galileo E5a, BeiDou B2a). L5 copes better with reflections between buildings.
No signalSatellites the chip expects above you from its orbit data, but does not hear (behind a building, a roof, the horizon).
AccuracyEstimated error radius of the position.
TTFFTime to first fix after the lock started.
Why other apps show bigger numbers: OsmAnd and many GPS apps count signals instead of satellites, and include the "no signal" ones in the total. Seen side by side, OsmAnd's 26/67 and GPS Locker's 15/22 can describe exactly the same sky.
6 · sky plot & signal strength

Sky plot

Looking up at the sky: the centre is straight overhead, the outer ring is the horizon, north is up. Each dot is a satellite.

filled dotused in the position fix
ringheard, but not used
dashedexpected there, no signal

GPS (G) · GLONASS (R) · BeiDou (C) · Galileo (E) · QZSS (J) · SBAS (S) · NavIC (I)

Signal strength

One column per satellite, in dB-Hz. The wide bar is L1, the narrow one next to it L5. Faded bars are not used in the fix. Rough guide: under 20 weak, 20 to 30 usable, over 30 good, over 40 open sky.

7 · tools - a-gps & cold reset
Refresh A-GPSAsks Android for fresh assistance data now: the exact time and the predicted orbits. Needs internet. Useful before a trip, or when the first fix is slow.
Cold resetDeletes all assistance data, then asks for a fresh copy. The next fix starts from nothing and can take longer. Only for a GPS that is stuck, shows a wrong place, or never gets a fix.
With a VPN or a DNS blocker, make sure the vendor's assistance servers are reachable (on Qualcomm phones *.xtracloud.net / *.izatcloud.net), otherwise the chip has no fresh orbit data and first fixes are slower.
8 · navigation apps

Android gives apps in the background a new position only every few minutes. A navigation app you switched away from then shows an old position for a moment when you come back.

Apps switched on here are exempt from that limit (Android's background location throttle whitelist), so they keep getting the position every second even in the background. OsmAnd, Waze, Sygic and Google Maps are on by default; other known navigation apps appear when they are installed.

Uninstalling the module puts the system list back as it was.

9 · permissions on hyperos / miui

The module grants every permission the app needs at install and at every boot: location (also in the background), notifications, no battery restrictions, autostart, and on Xiaomi phones the Millet exemption so the app is never frozen.

HyperOS and MIUI keep an extra permission layer for apps that were not installed from a store. It can block location and notifications silently: the tile works, but no satellites and no notification. Only the app's own settings page can change it.

  1. WebUI warning → Open settings (or press the module's Action button).
  2. Permissions → Location → Allow all the time.
  3. Notifications → on.

This is needed once. HyperOS keeps it, also through module updates.

10 · battery

A GPS receiver that never sleeps uses power: on a modern phone roughly as much as a navigation app with the screen off. That is fine in a car with a charger, not for a whole day in your pocket. Switch GPS Locker off when you no longer need it.

Lock after boot in Settings starts it automatically at every boot. Leave it off unless the phone lives in a car.

12 · command line

Everything the WebUI does goes through one script, which also works from a root shell (Termux):

su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh <command>'

Ready to copy:

su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh start'
su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh stop'
su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh status'
su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh check'
su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh fix'
su -c '/system/bin/sh /data/adb/modules/gps-locker/gpslocker.sh help'

13 · troubleshooting

The tile turns on, but no notification and no satellites

The system blocks the app. Run check; if it says blocked, follow section 9.

No tile in Quick Settings

Quick Settings → Edit → find GPS Locker in the list of available tiles and drag it up. After the app was reinstalled the tile has to be added again.

Searching for a long time

Indoors the phone may hear only a few weak satellites. Go outside or near a window. If it never finds a fix outdoors: Tools → Refresh A-GPS, and as a last step Cold reset.

Something else

Check the log:

su -c 'cat /data/adb/gps-locker/gps-locker.log'
14 · update & uninstall

Updating the module

Flash the new zip. The companion app is updated in place: settings, the tile and the permissions you allowed are kept.

Uninstall

Remove the module in your root manager and reboot. The companion app is removed, the battery and Millet exemptions are taken back, the app is unlocked in recents, the system's background location list is restored and /data/adb/gps-locker/ is deleted.
